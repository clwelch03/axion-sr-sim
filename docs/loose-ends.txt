1. D: the complex-frequency prescription is the highest priority.

The problematic assertion remains
\[
\widetilde\omega_{\mathbf q}
=\mathbf q\cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol\omega}.
\]
The signed combination correctly describes the real oscillation frequencies. It does not generally describe the growth rates.

For your existing example,
\[
\phi_1^2\phi_2^*
\propto
e^{-i(2\omega_1-\omega_2)t}
e^{(2\gamma_1+\gamma_2)t},
\]
so its complex frequency is
\[
2\widetilde\omega_1-\widetilde\omega_2^*,
\]
rather than \(2\widetilde\omega_1-\widetilde\omega_2\). Omiya explicitly uses the conjugated frequency for this driven channel. arxiv.org

But correcting this one sign is not a complete nonlinear prescription. On your evolving solution family,
\[
\dot F_{\mathbf q}
=
\sum_k\partial_{A_k}F_{\mathbf q}\,\dot A_k
+\partial_bF_{\mathbf q}\,\dot b
+\partial_jF_{\mathbf q}\,\dot j.
\]
There is generally no reason for this to equal
\((\mathbf q\cdot\boldsymbol\gamma)F_{\mathbf q}\).

You can see the assumption directly by differentiating a component:
\[
\partial_t^2\!\left(F_{\mathbf q}e^{-i\vartheta_{\mathbf q}}\right)
=e^{-i\vartheta_{\mathbf q}}
\left[
-\omega_{\mathbf q}^2F_{\mathbf q}
-2i\omega_{\mathbf q}\dot F_{\mathbf q}
-i\dot\omega_{\mathbf q}F_{\mathbf q}
+\ddot F_{\mathbf q}
\right].
\]
Replacing the frequency by \(\omega_{\mathbf q}+i\gamma_{\mathbf q}\) models a particular exponential envelope; it does not automatically represent the response of a deforming profile.

What needs resolving is therefore:
- What approximation do the solved \(\gamma_i\) represent?
- How does that approximation relate to the amplitude evolution obtained from action balance?
- Are errors in this prescription negligible at the order at which you calculate the fluxes and evolution?
The linear fixed-background check is \(\dot{\mathcal N}_i=2\gamma_i\mathcal N_i\), using the same time units. Passing it is necessary, but it does not test the interacting prescription.

Also, the integral phase definition is part of this same issue. For an evolving reconstruction, \(\dot\vartheta_i=\omega_i\) requires \(\vartheta_i=\int\omega_i\,dt\); it is not an optional improvement to \(\omega_i(t)t\). Using \(\omega_it\) as frozen-snapshot shorthand is entirely fine.

2. E: my earlier torus-averaging warning needs refinement for your actual two-mode system.

There are two distinct statements:
- Fourier projection on the abstract phase torus is mathematically well defined.
- That projection captures the physical slow dynamics without discarding a relevant relative phase.
Your periodicity argument establishes the first. The second requires checking which phase combinations survive physical averaging.

For an integer vector \(\mathbf k\),
\[
e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\xi}
=e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\omega)t}
e^{-i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf m)\varphi}.
\]
Azimuthal integration removes it unless
\[
\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf m=0.
\]
For the surviving combinations, averaging over fast time is justified when
\[
|\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\omega|\,T_{\rm slow}\gg1,
\]
for the combinations with dynamically relevant couplings. Small denominators and the existence of smooth multiphase solution families are recognized issues in general multiphase Whitham theory. Springer Nature Link

For \(211/322\), this is substantially less concerning than my original comment suggested. Since \(\mathbf m=(1,2)\), an azimuthally invariant combination must have
\[
\mathbf k=p(2,-1).
\]
Its frequency is \(p(2\omega_{211}-\omega_{322})\), ordinarily of order \(\mu\). The small difference \(\omega_{211}-\omega_{322}\) carries nonzero azimuthal number and is removed by azimuthal integration.

The question becomes more consequential with additional modes. For \(211,322,433\), for example,
\[
\psi=\xi_{211}-2\xi_{322}+\xi_{433}
\]
is azimuthally invariant, and its leading rest-mass frequency cancels. Its remaining detuning must be compared with the slow interaction/evolution rates. If it becomes sufficiently small, averaging over \(\psi\) can discard coherent dynamics, and you may need to retain it as a slow variable.

So I would justify the current two-mode averaging concisely, and make the arbitrary-\(K\) extension conditional on checking relevant resonances.

3. G: specify what the actions contain before using them to establish energy balance.

“Integrated over the cloud” remains insufficiently definite for a field containing propagating components.

For a real-frequency outgoing component,
\[
R_{\mathbf q}\sim \frac{C_{\mathbf q}e^{ikr}}r,
\]
your charge density per unit \(r\) approaches a nonzero constant. Its stationary radiation tail therefore contributes an amount growing with the outer integration radius.

A finite-domain action is perfectly legitimate. But you need to identify whether you use:
- the full field between finite extraction boundaries, or
- a localized cloud contribution with radiation accounted for separately.
Those choices affect the amplitude derivatives and the energy identities. In particular, “the cloud is contained” does not by itself eliminate contributions from the radiation crossing the domain.

This needs an implementation-specific definition and a domain-dependence check, not a long explanatory section.

4. H: separate closing the black-hole equations from proving equivalence to energy balance.

For black-hole evolution, supply the actual horizon-flux equations and their normalization. With physical fluxes defined positive toward increasing \(r\),
\[
\frac{dM_{\rm BH}}{dt}=-F_E^{\rm phys}(r_{\rm in}),
\qquad
\frac{dJ_{\rm BH}}{dt}=-F_J^{\rm phys}(r_{\rm in}),
\]
when the inner extraction flux represents the horizon flux. This gives mass loss when superradiant energy flows outward.

The more consequential issue is the conversion from instantaneous-\(M\) snapshot units to fixed-\(M_0\) evolution units. For example,
\[
\alpha=b\alpha_0,\qquad
\chi=\frac{j}{b^2},\qquad
M_0\omega_{\rm phys}=\frac{\widehat\omega}{b},
\]
where \(\widehat\omega=M_{\rm BH}\omega_{\rm phys}\).

Likewise, with the field normalization used in your displayed integrals, the physical action scales as
\[
\mathcal N_i^{\rm phys}
=f_a^2M_{\rm BH}^2\,\widehat{\mathcal N}_i
\]
in natural units. An invariant physical action is therefore not an invariant raw \(M=1\) integral when \(M\) changes. “All quantities use consistent fixed reference units” may accurately describe your code, but the conversion is important enough to show.

For the claimed equivalence to energy balance, the substantive condition is
\[
\boxed{
\partial_{A_k}E
=\sum_i\omega_i\,\partial_{A_k}\mathcal N_i
\quad\text{at fixed background}.
}
\]
This does not follow from the Legendre expression for \(E\) alone. It additionally needs the appropriate stationarity argument, including treatment of boundary terms. Your complex-frequency snapshots and open boundaries are precisely where that argument needs checking.

Define the residual
\[
D_k\equiv
\partial_{A_k}E-\sum_i\omega_i\partial_{A_k}\mathcal N_i.
\]
Then the chain rule gives
\[
\frac{dE}{d\tau}
-\sum_i\omega_i\frac{d\mathcal N_i}{d\tau}
=w+\sum_kD_k\frac{dA_k}{d\tau}.
\]
Thus your stated energy law requires the last term to vanish or be negligible at the retained order. Defining \(w\) through background derivatives does not establish that.

Your reported \(10^{-7}\) agreement could provide a strong check, depending on how independently the two formulations were evaluated. I would examine \(D_k\), its dependence on extraction boundaries, and whether energy and fluxes were calculated independently rather than reconstructed from the action identities.

I would work through D first, because its resolution determines what stationarity and accuracy claims you can use in H. The two-mode part of E now looks like a relatively short clarification; the arbitrary-mode extension deserves a separate resonance assessment.
