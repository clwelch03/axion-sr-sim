### Stiffness inferred from the log (an inference, not a measurement)

The rhs values were reconstructed from the stage states with the Tsit5 tableau (558 vectors from 93 steps) and
fitted linearly in (ln A₂₁₁, b, j).

- Fast eigenvalue λ = −3.2615e-8 per M₀, stable to 4 digits across six subsets.
- Eigenvector (δ ln A₂₁₁, δb, δj) = (1, −0.0448, −0.1510); δj/δb = 3.37.
- h·λ = 1.07527e8 × (−3.2615e-8) = −3.507. Tsit5's real-axis stability limit from its tableau is −3.5069. Tsit5 is
  on its stability boundary. This supports the hypothesis.
- Tsit5's accepted states sit off the slow manifold: d ln A₂₁₁/dt = +1.2286e-14 there. A stiff method that relaxes
  fully should give A₂₁₁ about 3.8e-7 (relative) higher than the Tsit5 log, with Im ω₂₁₁ ≈ 0. This is inside the
  1e-6 criterion but systematic.
- Im ω₂₁₁ depends on (a, b) only: ∂Im ω₂₁₁/∂a = +6.625e-7 at fixed b, ∂Im ω₂₁₁/∂b = −2.824e-7 at fixed a.
- The other eigenvalues are not constrained by the log. The finite-difference Jacobian is needed for them.

## Rosenbrock script

`/pscratch/sd/c/clwelch/stiff-integrator-test/stiff_spindown_run.jl`. Parses and loads; mesh matches 61102851
(690 nodes); reads `restart_state_t1.5e9.jls` written by the Jacobian agent. Bookkeeping tested with an analytic mock
rate function only.

Resolved versions in the scratch project: OrdinaryDiffEqRosenbrock 2.7.5, OrdinaryDiffEqDifferentiation 3.12.5,
OrdinaryDiffEqCore 4.19.1, SciMLBase 3.58.0, ADTypes 1.24.0, FiniteDiff 2.33.0. An offline resolve downgraded
OrdinaryDiffEqCore to 1.26.2; the online resolve is the one in use.

Design, where it differs from the brief:
- Hand-written central-difference `jac` with per-component steps `[1e-3, 1e-2, 1e-6, 1e-6]` for
  (ln A₂₁₁, ln A₃₂₂, b, j). Rate noise from two warm starts is about 1e-22, 3e-23, 2e-25, 8e-25. A perturbed solve
  at b + 1e-3 did not converge from the base solution, hence the small b and j steps.
- Every perturbed Jacobian solve is warm-started from the base-state solution.
- Rosenbrock23 is first-same-as-last in the installed version, so its accepted-step callback needs no extra solve.
  Rodas5P needs one.
- A failed stage solve rejects the step through `isoutofdomain`. A failed Jacobian solve is retried with the
  finite-difference step halved (twice), then the run stops with an error.
- Options: `--algorithm=Rosenbrock23|Rodas5P|Tsit5`, `--final-time`, `--initial-step`, `--tolerance`,
  `--fd-steps=`, `--difference=forward`, `--max-jac-age`, `--early-abort`, `--restart-solution`.

Commands (login node):

### Conservation

With `balance=:action`, the combination j + κ(N₁ + 2N₂) + κ∫(outer J flux)dt is constant by construction, where
N₁ and N₂ are the two wave actions and κ = (f_a/M_Pl)² = 0.217970. Define the residual

### Why b and a rise

|322⟩ extracts two units of J per quantum by its own linear superradiance, which pushes a below the |211⟩ threshold.
|211⟩ is then absorbed at the rate that keeps a on the threshold; the threshold rises with b, so a rises.

| Horizon channel at 3e12 | db/dt | da/dt part |
|---|---|---|
| \|211⟩ absorbed | +1.399e-15 | +2.42e-15 |
| \|322⟩ superradiant extraction | −3.72e-16 | −1.93e-15 |
| m = 0 component [2, −1] | +7.2e-18 | −1.3e-17 |

- Integrated to 3e12 (1e-8 run): |211⟩ returns 6.121e-4 in b and |322⟩ removes 1.678e-4; the net 4.4431e-4 equals
  Δb = 4.4431e-4. "Δb = κ × drop in |211⟩ occupation" alone is 38% too large.
- Holding a on a threshold of slope da/db = 0.426 requires 3.68 units of |211⟩ energy absorbed per unit of |322⟩
  energy extracted; the channel fluxes give 3.68, the integrated run 3.65. Δa/Δb = 0.4258 along the run.

  2 d ln A₃₂₂/dt = 2.263e-12.
- This differs from the f_a/M_Pl = 0.047 restart of 11-10, where the driver was absorption of the m = 0 component.
  Here that channel is 0.5% of the |211⟩ absorption.

### What limits the step after 2e12

Accuracy in ln A₂₁₁, not stability, solve failures or ln A₃₂₂. The scaled error per component on near-limit steps is
1.1–1.7 for ln A₂₁₁, 0.4–0.8 for j, 0.2–0.4 for b, under 0.1 for ln A₃₂₂. The forcing grows as A₃₂₂², so the step
shrinks as exp(−t/(3 × 4.4e11)): predicted factor 0.48 from 2.0e12 to 2.97e12, observed 0.46. No failed radial
solves in any run.

Rodas5P at 1e-7 took smaller steps than Rosenbrock23 at 1e-7 (3.6e10–8.8e10, no rejections) and is far more
accurate. Why a fifth-order method needs smaller steps here is not established; the forward-difference Jacobian in a

  gives a rejected step and a Jacobian recomputed at the same state, which loops down to the minimum step if the
  failure is deterministic.
- The scratch script's hand-written `jac` (per-component steps, defined failure policy, optional reuse), `tgrad`,
  and stored accepted-state record are the fuller version; moving them into `src/` would be the next library step.
- The diff has not been applied or run as written; the scratch script exercises the same logic outside `src/`.

## Fig. 9 scoping

Done (read-only scoping; estimates are labelled). Scratch scripts, logs and CSVs are in
`/pscratch/sd/c/clwelch/fig9-scoping/`. Paper: `docs/papers/2011.11646.pdf` (v2).

Notation: ε ≡ N/(GM²) is the paper's normalized occupation number; κ ≡ (f_a/M_Pl)² is the code's
`coupling_to_hole`; 𝒩 = ε/κ is the occupation in code units.

### Main conclusions

- All 12 curves are reachable with the paper's own two-level model. A scratch integration of the highlighted terms
  of Eqs. (34)–(36) with rounded Table I rates (`two_level_leading_order.py`, LSODA in ln ε, M fixed) ran all 12 in
  49 s and reproduces every qualitative feature.
- The figure was not made with the leading-order Table I rates: at α = 0.187 they make the first drop about 7× too
  fast (5e14 GeV, a = 0.78: 14 yr against about 1e2 yr digitized). Left panel: within a factor 1.5–3. Page 11 of the
  paper says the plots use "more accurate numerical or semi-analytic expressions" (App. B). That Fig. 9 is
  Eqs. (34)–(37) with such rates is an inference; the paper does not state it.
- In our units the cloud solution does not depend on f; f enters only through κ. One set of rates per panel serves
  all six curves. The existing α = 0.187 plateau scan with (M_Pl/f)² scaling already matches the first drop of the
  four small-f right-panel curves to 2–9% in time.
- Direct Tsit5 evolution is not viable for any full curve (estimates: 19 CPU-years for the 5e16 GeV right-panel
  curve, more for all others).
- The only curve that needs the nonlinear solver in time evolution is 5e16 GeV on the right (it overshoots before
  settling), and possibly 5e15–5e16 GeV on the left. The rest are quasi-equilibrium or free.

### Ingredients

In the code: |211⟩ and |322⟩ superradiance on exact Kerr, 211×211 → 322×BH, 322×322 → 211×∞, relativistic 3→1
emission, quartic potential (`QuarticPotential`, `src/potential.jl:61`; runs so far use cosine), BH mass evolution.

Not in the code: gravitational-wave annihilation and transitions (no such term in `src/`); cloud self-gravity.

Effect of the missing GW emission: for the right panel at f = 1e19 and 5e16 GeV the paper's |211⟩ cloud annihilates
in about 8 yr, long before |322⟩ grows. In our model it stays and is reabsorbed when the spin drops below the |211⟩
threshold (the spin-up seen in the α = 0.3 restart). The second drop still ends at the |322⟩ threshold but with a
shoulder and few-percent threshold shifts. Irrelevant for the left panel and for f ≤ 5e15 GeV.

### Cost with a stiff integrator (estimate)

300–600 accepted steps per curve, about 8–9 rhs per Rosenbrock23 step, about 20 s per rhs on the α = 0.187 mesh
(1355 nodes): roughly 14–28 h per right-panel curve. Left-panel curves cost several times more per rhs. The 5e12
and 1e11 GeV curves run to 6e21 M₀, where the needed rates (about 1.6e-22 per M₀) are at the rhs noise level.

### Existing scripts

`scripts/hydrogenic_spindown.jl` has leading-order superradiance rates, hydrogenic frequencies, and M and J
evolution. Missing for the paper's model: α as an argument (hard-coded 0.3 at line 26), the two quartic terms, GW
annihilation, a one-quantum seed, and a stiff solver. `scripts/leaver_spindown.py` is the free ODE with exact rates,
also with α = 0.3 hard-coded.

### Recommended order

1. Two-level driver for the paper's Eqs. (34)–(37) with Table I rates, α and f as arguments, ln ε state, stiff
   solver, one-quantum seed; overlay all 12 curves. Digitize the left panel and the right-panel second drops.
   Establishes that the model and regimes are right; will not match the right panel quantitatively.
2. Right panel with our rates: Leaver (N = 8000) for superradiance, and the two quartic coefficients measured at
   α = 0.187 on about 15 spins from 0.9 to 0.36. Check that the ODE's plateau reproduces 𝒩₂₁₁ = 491 and 𝒩₃₂₂ = 439
   at a = 0.9. Risk: the mesh and the (−1, 2) wave below a ≈ 0.65 are untested.
3. α = 0.06 feasibility: one Slurm job running `plateau_scan.jl 0.06 0.9` in a scratch copy with the tolerance
   raised. Tells us whether a two-mode nonlinear plateau converges and whether the k = 6e-4 outer data are usable.
4. Nonlinear evolution of the 5e16 GeV right-panel curve through its overshoot, only after the stiff-integrator
   result. Add a GW annihilation term if the 1e19 and 5e16 GeV second drops must match in detail.

Biggest risks: the convergence floor for two-mode nonlinear solves at small α (untested); the near-threshold
(−1, 2) wave; missing GW emission for the two large-f right-panel curves; not knowing exactly which rates the paper
used.
