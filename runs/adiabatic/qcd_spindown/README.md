# QCD axion spin-down: 20 solar masses

Started October 8, 2026, at the user's explicit request to complete a first
spin-versus-time graph directly. This is separate from the paused local
alpha=0.42 integrator draft. The model retains a nonlinear cosine-potential
211 primary, a linear 322 cloud on its phase-averaged background, and the
driven m=0 and m=3 scalar fields. BH mass and angular momentum both evolve.

## Inputs and time origin

- Initial BH mass: 20 solar masses; spin: 0.99.
- Axion rest energy: 1e-12 eV, giving initial alpha = 0.14966276123144445.
- The QCD relation `m_a = 5.7 micro-eV * (1e12 GeV / f_a)` gives
  `f_a = 5.7e18 GeV`; see [Grilli di Cortona et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02867).
  The implementation retains the repository's cosine potential, rather than
  implementing the full zero-temperature chiral QCD potential.
- `epsilon = (f_a / M_Planck)^2 = 0.21796991094505996`, with unreduced Planck mass.
- Initial code energies: `e_211 = 1e-5`, `e_322 = 1e-8`, corresponding to
  physical energies `2.1796991e-6 M0` and `2.1796991e-9 M0`.
  Time zero is these small **classical** clouds, not occupation one.
  Omiya's dimensionless primary energy cannot be copied literally here without
  making the initial physical cloud much heavier at the QCD decay constant.

## Implementation

[`qcd_stationary_point.jl`](../../../src/adiabatic/drivers/qcd_stationary_point.jl)
composes the existing stationary solvers and saves one independent checkpoint
per target. It inverts primary energy by correcting the finite-amplitude
stationary solution, solves the averaged 322 eigenproblem, and solves both
driven fields. An additional primary/secondary solve at the **same** alpha and
spin measures `partial omega2 / partial E1`. This is not a derivative along
the changing BH trajectory.

[`qcd_spindown.py`](../../../src/adiabatic/qcd_spindown.py) implements the leading
instantaneous-background balance in
[`coupled-bh-cloud-balance.md`](../../../docs/coupled-bh-cloud-balance.md).
It evolves BH mass/angular losses, logarithmic cloud energies, and accumulated
outgoing scalar energy in fixed initial-BH units. Channel-by-channel projection
avoids numerical cancellation when extracting the tiny secondary rate.

The table has 21 points on a pilot path in primary energy, alpha, and spin.
The pilot is used to place stationary solves, not to set their growth rates.
The ODE uses factored cubic interpolation of those numerical inputs. It restores
the horizon frequency factor using the **actual evolving spin**, monitors
departures from the pilot background, and rejects extrapolation. Fresh solves
on the resulting trajectory are required to check interpolation and the small
background discrepancy. This is a local interpolation along one trajectory,
not a reusable three-dimensional stationary table.

The nonrelativistic comparison evolves the same BH variables and initial cloud
energies, using the repository's signed hydrogenic 211 and 322 growth rates,
with `omega_R = mu` consistently in its horizon factor and flux ratios.
Its clouds have no self-interaction or driven scalar channels. Both rates are
updated as alpha and spin change. The plotted rate convention is energy growth
`2 omega_I`; there is no amplitude/occupation factor-of-two ambiguity.

## Numerical settings and reproduction

Primary harmonics: `(1,1), (3,1), (5,1), (3,3), (5,3), (5,5)`;
secondary: `(2,2), (4,2), (6,2)`; driven m=0: l=0,2,4;
driven m=3: l=3,5,7. Angular quadrature: 12 polar by 25 azimuthal points.
Tortoise interval: -100 to 2000, match at 40, primary spacing 0.25 M.
The secondary/driven solvers use twice the primary spacing with aligned RK
stages. Primary matching tolerance: 1e-7 in residual/A; secondary: 1e-5 in
its unit-outer-amplitude normalization. Actual residuals are saved per point.
Primary energy inversion tolerance: 1e-6 relative.

## Physical field and perturbative-amplitude check (October 12, 2026)

Use capital `Phi` for the canonically normalized, dimensionful field and
lowercase `phi = Phi/fa` for the code's dimensionless real field. Thus
`Phi[GeV] = 5.7e18 * phi`.
The saved `peak_field` is already reconstructed with the complex-conjugate
partners and all retained primary harmonics. It is not the outer coefficient
`amplitude` and needs no extra factor of two or radial rescaling.

For the cosine potential, the magnitude of the cubic source relative to the
linear mass term is

$$\frac{|(m_a^2/f_a^2)\Phi^3/6|}{|m_a^2\Phi|}
  =\frac{\Phi^2}{6f_a^2}=\frac{\phi^2}{6}.$$

Consequently the very small vacuum coupling `lambda = 3.08e-80` does not by
itself ensure weak classical self-interaction. The potential's small-field
expansion requires `|Phi|/fa << 1`, not comparison of a dimensionful field with
`ma/fa`. Even below unity, self-interaction can affect weak binding or accumulate
over long times; this check is not a proof of uniform perturbative convergence.
This field convention agrees with [Omiya et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.04382v3),
whose adiabatic method solves the nonlinear potential without expanding in
the self-interaction.

The post-processing driver interpolates `peak_field / sqrt(code_energy)`
along the existing stationary track and restores `sqrt(code_energy(t))` using
the evolving BH mass. It does not extrapolate or rerun the evolution.

## Scope of the result

This first trajectory is scalar-only and uses leading instantaneous-Kerr
balance. GW emission, self-gravity feedback, metric work, cloud angular-momentum
background derivatives, accretion, mutual nonlinear cloud deformation, and
additional independent clouds are absent. Numerical convergence within this
model does not validate those omitted physical effects. In particular, the
final cloud may contain several percent of the BH mass: this is a preliminary
evolution under the stated approximation, not precision astrophysical timing.

The 322 cloud remains explicitly evolved even when it is too small to visibly
change the first spin-down episode. A later, secondary-dominated epoch would
require a new stationary track and a fresh check of the approximation that the
secondary remains linear on a primary-controlled background.
